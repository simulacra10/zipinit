* Purpose

This document defines the philosophical foundation of ZIP Apportionment.

It is intentionally separate from implementation details, legal analysis,
and policy mechanics. Its purpose is to answer one question:

**What problem is ZIP Apportionment actually trying to solve?**

* Representation Definition

** The Properties of Representation

Political representation must possess four minimum properties.

*** 1. Constituency

There must be an identifiable body of people on whose behalf political power is exercised.

Without a defined constituency, there is no answer to the question:

#+begin_quote
Whom does this representative represent?
#+end_quote

*** 2. Authorization

The constituency must have a recognized means of selecting, approving, or rejecting the representative.

In an electoral system, authorization normally occurs through voting.

*** 3. Agency

The representative must possess actual authority to speak, vote, deliberate, or act on behalf of the constituency.

A person without institutional authority may be an advocate or spokesperson, but not a political representative in the full sense.

*** 4. Accountability

The constituency must have a mechanism for evaluating and replacing the representative.

Authorization establishes the representative relationship. Accountability maintains it.

*** Summary
These are the minimum properties of political representation. Representative governments, both past and present, operate through some combination of constituency, authorization, agency, and accountability.

However, these properties only establish that representation exists. They do not tell us whether the representation is clear, stable, fair, or resistant to political manipulation.

That requires representational integrity.

** The Properties of Representational Integrity

*** What Is Representational Integrity?

Representational integrity is the degree to which a political system preserves a clear, stable, understandable, and accountable relationship between a community and the person empowered to represent it.

Representational integrity extends and strengthens the minimum properties of representation.

It does not replace constituency, authorization, agency, or accountability. Instead, it asks whether those properties operate in a way that faithfully connects people, communities, and representatives.

Representational integrity can be understood through four broad properties:

1. Community connection
2. Continuity
3. Transparency
4. Political responsibility

*** 1. Community Connection

Representation should begin with communities that people can recognize and understand.

A citizen should be able to identify the community being represented, understand why they belong to it, and know who represents it.

Community connection includes three related qualities:

**** Identifiability

Citizens should be able to identify their constituency and the representative responsible for it.

A constituency should not exist only as lines on a technical map. It should make sense to the people who live within it.

**** Community Fidelity

The constituency should correspond as closely as practical to the community people actually inhabit.

People form communities through shared places, institutions, infrastructure, businesses, schools, churches, roads, and recurring local concerns.

Community fidelity measures how closely the political constituency reflects those lived relationships.

**** Granularity

Representation should operate at a scale small enough to preserve meaningful differences between communities.

When many distinct communities are combined into one large district, their individual interests become less visible.

Greater granularity gives representation greater local resolution.

*** 2. Continuity

The relationship between a community and its representative should remain recognizable over time.

Citizens should not be repeatedly moved between constituencies because political officials redraw boundaries.

Continuity includes two related qualities:

**** Stability

The basic constituency should remain substantially recognizable over time.

Population changes may require adjustments, but those adjustments should not require the complete reconstruction of political geography after every census.

**** Traceability

Citizens should be able to follow the relationship between themselves, their community, their constituency, and their representative.

That relationship should be direct and understandable:

#+begin_quote
Citizen → Community → Constituency → Representative
#+end_quote

A citizen should be able to determine why they belong to a particular constituency and how that constituency produces their representation.

*** 3. Transparency

The rules that create representation should be public, understandable, and independently verifiable.

Citizens should not be required to trust political parties, commissions, specialists, or mapmakers without being able to examine the process for themselves.

Transparency includes three related qualities:

**** Rule Determinacy

The composition of constituencies should follow a public and predictable rule rather than the discretionary judgment of political actors.

The same rule should apply regardless of which party controls the government.

**** Reproducibility

Different people applying the same rules to the same public data should reach the same result.

A trustworthy system should not merely announce a map or allocation. Citizens, journalists, researchers, and political opponents should be able to independently verify it.

**** Legibility

The basic logic of the system should be understandable in ordinary language.

Citizens do not need to perform every calculation themselves, but they should be able to understand how their constituency was determined and why they have the representation they do.

*** 4. Political Responsibility

Representation should create a clear relationship of responsibility between a community and its representative.

The representative should know which community must be served, and the community should know whom it can reward, challenge, or replace.

Political responsibility includes two related qualities:

**** Accountability Precision

Citizens should know which representative is responsible for their community.

Representatives should know which community has the authority to judge their performance.

Accountability is strongest when the relationship is clear:

#+begin_quote
This is the community.

This is its representative.

This is the community to which that representative must answer.
#+end_quote

**** Resistance to Manipulation

The system should minimize opportunities for politicians, parties, commissions, or incumbents to construct constituencies for their own advantage.

No political system can eliminate manipulation entirely. A system with representational integrity limits where discretion can be exercised and makes manipulation easier for the public to recognize.

** Representation and Representational Integrity

The four minimum properties of representation answer the question:

#+begin_quote
Does representation exist?
#+end_quote

The four properties of representational integrity answer a different question:

#+begin_quote
How well does that representation connect a community to its representative?
#+end_quote

A political system may provide representation while possessing little representational integrity.

It may provide a constituency, but construct that constituency around political interests.

It may provide authorization, but dilute a community's voice within a much larger district.

It may provide agency, but place political power too far from the people being represented.

It may provide accountability, but spread responsibility so broadly that no particular community can exercise it effectively.

Representational integrity therefore rests on four principles:

1. The constituency should reflect a recognizable community.
2. The relationship should remain stable over time.
3. The system should operate through understandable and verifiable rules.
4. The representative should remain clearly responsible to the community.

These principles provide a practical standard for judging any representative system.

The question is not merely whether people have a representative.

The question is whether the structure preserves the integrity of the relationship between the people, their community, and the person who acts in their name.

*** Summary
ZIP apportionment is not merely another way to draw districts. It extends the functional properties of representation. It makes representation more geographically precise, more stable, more reproducible, and more closely anchored to recognizable communities.

** A Different Premise

Working idea:

Political actors should not possess broad discretion to create the
geography through which representation is determined.

* First Principles

** Principle 1 — Representation begins with stable communities

Notes:

Communities are rarely arbitrary. More often than not they emerge naturally around something—a church, an industry, a crossroads, a harbor, or some other common gathering place. Over time, a culture grows from those shared experiences. Schools, restaurants, local businesses, sports teams, civic organizations, and neighborhood traditions all reinforce the identity of the community and the bonds between the people who live there.

ZIP codes are one of the easiest ways to see this bond. They are not what creates the community; they are simply one of the clearest ways to recognize where many communities already exist

* The Design Objective

** Representational Integrity

Working definition:

Representational Integrity exists when a representative system:

- is based upon externally defined geography
- maintains constitutionally acceptable population equality
- minimizes discretionary political decisions
- applies deterministic rules
- remains transparent and publicly auditable
- produces stable, recognizable communities

Relationship:

Representational Integrity is the objective.

* Why ZIPs?

Candidate characteristics:

- Familiar to citizens
- Already exist
- Non-political origin
- Publicly available
- Reasonably stable
- Already used by government
- Already used by the Census
- Easily recognizable communities

For each characteristic:

- Why does it matter?
- Are there exceptions?
- Could another predefined geography satisfy the same requirement?

* What ZIP Apportionment Is

- A proposal for predetermined geographic apportionment.
- A system that begins with existing communities.
- A method that sharply limits discretionary map drawing.
- A framework for achieving Representational Integrity.

* What ZIP Apportionment Is Not

- Not simply "one representative per ZIP."
- Not an argument against population equality.
- Not opposition to independent commissions.
- Not opposition to ranked-choice voting.
- Not a federal proposal by definition.
- Not a proposal about repealing the Seventeenth Amendment.
- Not a claim that manipulation becomes impossible.
