Friends,

Preliminary reports indicate that the Maryland General Assembly has passed HB 2100, the proposed constitutional amendment that would weaken Maryland’s existing limits on mid-decade congressional redistricting.

The measure must still be approved by Maryland voters before it can take effect. That means the legislative fight may be over, but the public campaign is just beginning.

ZIP Apportionment will be joining Republicans and other Marylanders in opposing this amendment. We do so without surrendering our independence or reducing the issue to partisan loyalty.

Our objection is simple: politicians should not be permitted to rewrite constitutional restraints merely because those restraints interfere with their immediate political objectives.

Supporters of HB 2100 will argue that Maryland must be able to respond when Republican-controlled states redraw their maps for partisan advantage. That argument may be politically understandable, but it is constitutionally dangerous. The misconduct of one political party does not justify granting another political party the same power.

Gerrymandering does not become legitimate when the preferred side does it.

The amendment would further normalize the idea that congressional districts are partisan weapons, to be redesigned whenever political leaders believe they can gain an advantage. Under that system, voters do not meaningfully choose their representatives. Legislators and party officials choose which voters will be placed into each representative’s district.

That reverses the proper relationship between the citizen and the government.

Maryland needs stronger representational integrity, not fewer restraints on partisan mapmaking. A representative should be accountable to an identifiable community with understandable boundaries and durable interests. Districts should not be engineered through sophisticated data analysis to predetermine election outcomes or protect political power.

This is why ZIP Apportionment has consistently argued that the real problem is structural. Independent commissions, judicial review, and promises of bipartisan restraint may reduce certain abuses, but they do not resolve the underlying incentive. As long as political representation is built around artificial districts that must be repeatedly redrawn, those controlling the process will face enormous pressure to manipulate it.

HB 2100 moves Maryland in the wrong direction. Rather than restraining that incentive, it removes an obstacle standing in its way.

I recently submitted written testimony opposing the amendment during the General Assembly’s public comment period. That testimony is now part of the official record, but its argument must reach far beyond Annapolis.

Over the coming months, Maryland voters will need to understand what they are actually being asked to approve. This is not merely a technical change to redistricting procedure. It is a proposal to give current and future political majorities greater control over the structure of congressional elections.

We should oppose that power regardless of which party expects to benefit from it today.

I hope you will follow this issue, discuss it with your friends and neighbors, and vote against HB 2100 when it appears on the ballot.

Politicians should not choose their voters. Voters should choose their representatives.


Norman Bauer
Director - ZIP Apportionment Initiative
